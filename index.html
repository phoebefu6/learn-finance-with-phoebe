In month 10, Marchmont Instruments earned £52,781. It was the best month the company had ever had, every month before it had also been profitable, and nobody had made a mistake. In the same month the bank balance went to minus £7,432. Both numbers are correct and both come out of the same set of books. This is a course about reading a company's numbers well enough to see that coming: what the three statements are each for, why profit and cash pull apart, what growth actually costs, and how to size the gap and ask for the money. Six leader sessions with no spreadsheets, ten practitioner sessions building a real three-statement model, and a bench that runs the whole thing in your browser so every figure can be checked rather than believed.
