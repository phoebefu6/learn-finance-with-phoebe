Profit is an opinion, cash is a fact

That sentence is an old saying in finance. It was worn smooth by traders, auditors and credit analysts long before it reached this room, and it is repeated because it keeps turning out to be useful. Marchmont Instruments sells laboratory instruments to labs that pay on 75 day terms. The moment an instrument ships, the sale has been earned and the profit is recorded. The money turns up about two and a half months later. Multiply that one gap across a year of growing sales and twelve months of trading produced £445,631 of net income and minus £297,261 of free cash flow. Nobody lied, nothing was stolen, and both figures came out of the same books. Tonight is about why a profit figure is assembled out of judgement calls, and why a bank balance is not.

Part 0

Two numbers that cannot both be the headline

Session 1 put the three statements side by side and left one question open: why do profit and cash separate at all? The answer is a single accounting convention, and once it is visible the rest of the course follows from it. Tonight covers what an accrual is in plain words, the four judgement calls that sit inside every profit figure, and the sense in which the cash figure is harder to argue with. The example is Marchmont Instruments throughout. Session 3 follows the money and shows exactly where the difference went.

★ What you walk out with tonight. A one sentence definition of an accrual you can use with anybody. The four places judgement legitimately enters a profit figure, so you can ask about them by name rather than suspecting something vague. The Marchmont twelve month pair, £445,631 of net income against minus £297,261 of free cash flow, as the size of what a timing convention can do. And the honest limit on the saying: the cash figure has less room for opinion, not none.

LiveAn accrual, in one sentence4 min▶

An accrual means recording an event in the period it happens rather than the period the money moves. A sale is recorded when the goods are delivered and the customer owes for them, not when the payment clears. A cost is recorded when it is incurred, not when the supplier is paid. That is the whole idea.

The reason is that a period has to mean something. Without accruals, a month in which a big customer happened to pay would look like a triumph and the month it was earned would look empty, and the accounts would describe the timing of bank transfers rather than the trading. Accruals exist to make a period comparable to the one before it.

Real world

Marchmont ships an instrument on the first of the month. The sale is earned that day, so the revenue and the profit on it land in that month. The lab pays on 75 day terms, so the money reaches the bank around the middle of the third month after. Both facts are recorded. They are recorded in different places, on different dates.

Self-studyWhere the saying comes from, and what it is not saying4 min▶

The phrase has circulated in finance for decades and has been attributed to several people, which usually means nobody can prove who said it first. It is a working maxim rather than a discovery, and it is worth handling as one.

It is not an accusation. Calling profit an opinion does not mean a profit figure is unreliable, invented or negotiable. It means a profit figure is the output of a set of defensible choices, made in good faith, that a different team could have made differently and still been right. It is also not a claim that cash is beyond question: the amount in the account is not in dispute, but which section of a statement a movement belongs in still is.

Part 1 · covers accruals

One sale, two dates

10 min live

Profit and cash separate for one reason, and it is visible in a single transaction. The sale has an earning date and a payment date, and at Marchmont those are 75 days apart.

LiveWhy the gap compounds instead of cancelling4 min▶

One sale on 75 day terms is only a delay. If the business sold the same amount every month for ever, the money from an older sale would arrive as the newer one shipped, and the delay would stop mattering after the first few months.

It stops cancelling the moment sales grow. Each month Marchmont ships more than it shipped 75 days ago, so the money arriving is always smaller than the money owed, and the shortfall widens every month. Marchmont grows 4% a month, holds 60 days of stock and pays its own suppliers in 30 days, and the result over twelve months is £445,631 of net income against minus £297,261 of free cash flow. Session 4 is entirely about that mechanism.

LiveThe two ways an accrual shows up3 min▶

Money owed to the business by customers is recorded as an asset, because the sale was earned and the payment is expected. Money the business owes its suppliers is recorded as a liability for the same reason in reverse. Both are accruals, and both sit on the balance sheet waiting to turn into cash in one direction or the other.

The asymmetry matters. Marchmont waits 75 days to be paid and pays its own suppliers in 30, so it is funding the difference out of its own bank balance on every single sale. That is not a mistake. It is a commercial position, and it has a price, which session 3 measures.

Self-studyRevenue recognition when the sale is not one event4 min▶

Shipping a boxed instrument has an obvious date. Most sales do not. A contract with an installation phase, a calibration visit, a twelve month support wrap and an option to extend does not have a single moment at which the business has done what it promised. The revenue has to be split across the things promised, and the split is a judgement.

This is where honest teams diverge most visibly. The same contract, read by two competent accountants with the same rulebook, can produce different revenue in the current quarter and the same total across the life of the contract. Neither is wrong. The total is fixed and the timing is argued about, which is precisely what "opinion" means here.

Part 2 · covers the judgement calls

Four decisions inside one number

7 min live

A profit figure is not measured, it is assembled. Four choices do most of the assembling, and every one of them is made in good faith by people who could reasonably have chosen otherwise.

LiveWhy none of this is dishonest4 min▶

Each of the four choices exists because the alternative is worse. Refusing to recognise revenue until every obligation in a three year contract is complete would make the first two years look dead. Refusing to capitalise anything would make the year a factory is built look catastrophic and every year afterwards look effortless. Estimates are not a weakness in the system, they are the system doing its job, which is to describe a period fairly rather than to record bank transfers.

What follows from that is a habit, not a suspicion. The useful move is to ask which judgements a figure rests on and whether they changed since last year, because a changed judgement moves profit without anything happening in the business.

LiveThe limit of the saying3 min▶

Cash has less room for opinion, not none. The balance in the account is not arguable. What is arguable is presentation: which section of the cash flow statement a movement is reported in, and what is subtracted before a figure is called "free". Two honest preparers can put the same payment in different sections and produce cash flow statements that look different while describing identical bank activity.

So the saying is a comparison, not an absolute. Profit carries several layers of estimate about the future. Cash carries one layer of classification about the past. That is a real difference in kind and it is why the maxim survived, but a cash figure presented without saying what is in it is still a figure worth questioning.

Self-studyWhere practitioners genuinely disagree4 min▶

There is a long running argument about whether accrual profit or cash generation is the better description of how a business performed, and it has not been settled. One camp holds that accruals are the whole point, because they match effort to result and a cash figure can be flattered for a quarter by simply not paying anybody. The other holds that estimates accumulate quietly, that the estimates are made by people with an interest in the answer, and that cash generation is the measure that cannot be talked into existence.

Both positions have decades of evidence behind them and both have famous failures on the other side: businesses that reported profit for years and collapsed for want of cash, and businesses judged harshly on cash while building something that only accruals could show. The practical position most experienced readers end up at is not a winner but a rule of use. Read them as a pair, and treat a large or widening divergence as the thing to explain rather than as a verdict on either number.

Part 3 · build-along

Take a profit figure apart

20 min hands on

No spreadsheet. Six steps, done out loud, half on Marchmont and half on a number from your own organisation.

Give one sale two dates

Pick a real sale you know about. Write the date the work was done or the goods were delivered, and the date the money actually reached the account. If those are the same date, you are in a cash business and much of tonight will not bite. If they are weeks apart, write the number of days down, because that number is what separates your profit from your bank balance.

Say the accrual out loud

In one sentence, with no jargon: an accrual records the event when it happens rather than when the money moves. Say it to somebody who does not work in finance and see whether it survives. If it needs a second sentence, the second sentence is that it applies to costs the same way.

Name all four judgements

From memory: when revenue is recognised, how stock is valued, what is capitalised rather than expensed, and how long an asset is expected to last. Then find which of the four applies hardest in your own organisation. In most service businesses it is the first. In most manufacturers it is the second.

Move one judgement and predict the direction

Marchmont writes equipment off over 60 months. Say which way this year's profit moves if the life is set to 36 months instead, and confirm that not a single pound of cash changes hands differently. Then do the same for a decision to expense something that had been capitalised.

Size the Marchmont pair

Twelve months of net income was £445,631 and free cash flow was minus £297,261. Say the pair out loud as one sentence a chief executive would accept, without using the word "but". The sentence that works usually starts with the fact that both are true.

Find the opinion still hiding in the cash figure

Cash has less room for judgement, not none. Name the one place it still enters, and say what you would ask to close it. The answer is about which section a movement is reported in and what has been subtracted before somebody calls the result "free".

The habit to keep. When a profit figure moves and nothing in the business feels different, ask whether a judgement moved. A changed depreciation life, a changed stock write down policy or a changed view on when a contract is earned will all move profit on their own, and none of them will show up in the bank.

Check yourself

Three questions

Marchmont ships an instrument today to a lab on 75 day terms. What is recorded today?

That is what an accrual is: the event is recorded in the period it happens rather than the period the money moves. It is also exactly why profit and cash separate, and across twelve months at Marchmont the separation reached £445,631 of net income against minus £297,261 of free cash flow.

Two competent finance teams are given the same twelve months of the same company and report different profit. What is the most likely explanation?

A profit figure is assembled out of choices rather than measured. Different defensible choices produce different profit from identical transactions, which is the whole meaning of calling profit an opinion. None of the four judgements moves the bank balance by a penny.

In what sense is cash a fact?

The saying is a comparison, not an absolute. Profit carries layers of estimate about the future and cash carries one layer of classification about the past. A cash figure presented without saying what has been subtracted from it is still worth questioning.

Before session 3

Homework

Twenty minutes, on your own organisation or any company whose accounts are public.

Find the judgements written down

Published accounts state the significant accounting policies and the estimates management considers most uncertain. Find that section and read the two or three items listed. Those are the judgements the preparers themselves think could most change the profit figure.

Ask where your own money is

Write down what your organisation's customers owe it right now, and how long they typically take to pay. If nobody can answer the second part in under a minute, write that down instead. Session 3 turns both answers into a cash figure.
