Nine sessions built a model one line at a time. This one puts the whole thing in front of you and lets you drive it. LiveWhat the bench is actually doing4 min▶

Each press of the button runs the model month by month for the horizon chosen. For every month it computes the income statement from the assumptions, works out the three working capital balances from that month's trading, turns the change in them into a cash movement, and lands the results on the balance sheet through the two wires from session 1. Then it checks that the sheet ties.

None of the figures below are stored. Change a control and the previous answer is discarded and a new one computed, which is why an assumption you thought was minor can move the headline more than you expected.

Part 1 · the bench

Drive it

15 min hands on

Four controls. Start with the base case as it stands, then change one thing at a time and predict the direction before you press the button. Predicting first is the whole exercise: an assumption you cannot predict the sign of is one you do not yet understand.

LiveThe four runs worth doing first5 min▶
  • Base case, 24 months. Cash goes negative in month 10. Note the net income column beside it and confirm it never turns negative.
  • Base case, 48 months. Same assumptions, longer window, and the company comes back. The trough is minus £115,501 in month 19 and it is above zero again in month 28. Nothing changed except how far you looked.
  • Growth at 0%, 24 months. The gap disappears entirely and twelve month net income falls to £165,627. Growing less makes the company safer and poorer at the same time.
  • Customers pay in 45 days, 24 months. The gap disappears and twelve month net income stays at £445,631, exactly. Not approximately: the timing levers do not touch the income statement at all.
Self-studyTwo combinations worth finding4 min▶

First, set growth to 6% and the horizon to 48 months. The trough is minus £897,962 in month 31 and the company does not recover inside the window. Between 4% and 6% the problem stops being a bridge and becomes something the business cannot outgrow, and there is no warning label on the control to tell you that.

Second, put growth at 8% and leave the horizon at 12 months. Everything looks survivable, because at twelve months it is. The window is doing the reassuring, not the business.

Part 2 · the check

Break it on purpose

8 min live

Press "Break the tie". The button drops one side of a single transaction in month 7, the way a real modelling mistake does, and leaves everything else alone. Look at what happens to the page.

LiveWhy a check you never see fail is not a check4 min▶

A tie test that has only ever reported success tells you nothing, because a test that cannot fail and a test that is passing look identical from the outside. The habit worth keeping is to break the thing deliberately, once, and confirm the check notices. Session 5 did that to the model; this button does it on demand.

The same applies to any check you inherit. Before relying on somebody else's validation, make it fail once on purpose. If you cannot make it fail, you have not found a robust check, you have found a decoration.

Self-studyWhat ties and what does not3 min▶

A tying balance sheet says the bookkeeping is internally consistent. It says nothing at all about whether the assumptions are sensible. A model can tie perfectly every month and still assume a growth rate nobody can deliver and a collection period nobody has agreed to.

That is the honest limit of the check and it is worth stating whenever the check is quoted as reassurance. Internal consistency is necessary and it is nowhere near sufficient.

Part 3 · build-along

Size the gap and write the ask

11 min hands on

Six steps, on the bench above, ending in four sentences you could send to somebody who controls money.

Find the trough, not the first bad month

Base case, 48 month horizon. Write down the first negative month and the deepest point. They are nine months and £108,069 apart, and only one of them is the number to finance.

Find the end

Read the month the cash position comes back above zero. That month is what turns the request into a term rather than an open-ended overdraft, and it is the single fact that makes the ask answerable.

Test the assumption that sized it

Move growth to 6% and run it again. The trough moves by an order of magnitude and the recovery disappears. Write down what that means for the confidence you can honestly attach to the first figure.

Price the alternative

Put growth back and set customers paying in 45 days instead. The gap closes without borrowing anything. Note what that change would actually cost in the real world, which the model does not know and will never tell you.

Break it and look away

Press the break button, then read the table as if you had just been handed it. Decide how long it would have taken you to notice without the check. That number is why the check exists.

Write the four sentences

How much, over what period, resting on which assumption, and what you will do if that assumption turns out wrong. Four sentences, no spreadsheet attached. If any of them needs a paragraph, the model has not finished telling you something.

The habit to keep. Report the horizon next to the answer, every time. A forecast without the window it was run over is not a forecast, it is a number, and the same model over two windows supported two opposite decisions in session 9.

Check yourself

Three questions

The base case shows cash first going negative in month 10 at minus £7,432. What should the funding request be built on?

A · £7,432, because that is the shortfall the model reports at the first point of failure
B · The trough of minus £115,501 in month 19, plus a justified buffer, over a term set by the recovery in month 28
C · The twelve month free cash flow of minus £297,261, because that is the total cash consumed
The first negative month understates the need by more than a hundred thousand pounds. Funding it covers month 10 and nothing after: the balance is -£28,360 in month 11 and -£47,261 in month 12. The trough is the depth of the hole and the recovery month is what gives the facility a term.

You press "Break the tie" and the check reports a failure. What is the status of the other figures on the page?

A · Still reliable, because only the balance sheet was affected and the income statement is computed separately
B · Reliable up to month 7 and unreliable after it, so the early months can still be quoted
C · Not trustworthy, because a sheet that does not tie means a transaction was recorded on one side only and nothing downstream can be relied on
The figures all still render and still look plausible, which is exactly the danger. An imbalance means the bookkeeping is internally inconsistent, and the check is the only thing on the page reporting it.

A model ties in every month across every run you have tried. What does that establish?

A · That the bookkeeping is internally consistent, and nothing whatever about whether the assumptions are sensible
B · That the forecast is reliable, since the statements agree with each other
C · That the assumptions have been validated, because inconsistent assumptions would break the tie
Internal consistency is necessary and nowhere near sufficient. A model can tie perfectly while assuming a growth rate nobody can deliver and a collection period nobody agreed to.
After the course

Homework

One hour, on a business you actually know.

Build the smallest version

Revenue, gross margin, fixed and variable costs, and the three working capital dials. Six inputs is enough to produce the effect this whole course has been about, and anything more can wait.

Put a tie check in before you put a chart in

Then break it once on purpose and confirm it fires. A check you have never seen fail is not yet a check.

Run it over two horizons

Whatever window feels natural, and then double it. If the two disagree, the longer one is usually the one telling you something, and either way the horizon goes next to the answer.
